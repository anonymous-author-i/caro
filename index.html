Abstract

We propose CARO, a contact-agnostic residual observation framework for robust quadruped locomotion. CARO embeds a fixed-base Euler–Lagrange model into the reinforcement learning control loop to construct a torque-level residual without torque sensors, explicit contact estimation, or floating-base position and linear-velocity measurements. A disturbance observer extracts structured feedback on dynamics mismatch, which the policy uses for online adaptation. CARO shares the nominal policy’s terrain, command, and domain-randomization settings and requires neither a specialized disturbance curriculum nor additional adaptation supervision. Simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate improved zero-shot robustness to out-of-distribution payloads, center-of-mass shifts, terrain geometries, abrupt dynamics changes, and elevated-platform landings.

Method

A fixed-base internal model turns joint measurements and commanded torques into a structured residual. The observer supplies this feedback to the policy, which learns how to respond to changes in dynamics and contact conditions.

What does the policy observe?

The joint-level residuals change after a payload step, providing feedback on aggregate dynamics mismatch across all four legs.

The signal captures the combined effects of contact, loading, and model mismatch. It does not identify individual external forces or payload mass, and the policy uses it as an observation rather than direct torque compensation.

Real-world Results

Zero-shot deployment on the DeepRobotics Lite3, without hardware-specific policy fine-tuning.

Maintaining heading under a center-of-mass shift

With a 2.5 kg payload attached laterally, CARO restores a near-level body attitude and maintains the commanded heading. Vanilla develops persistent roll and heading deviation.

Overlaid poses show temporal progression within each trial.

Walking with an 8.5 kg payload

CARO maintains forward locomotion with an 8.5 kg payload. Vanilla falls in the illustrated 6.5 kg trial.

Recovering from a platform landing

Released from a 0.2 m platform, CARO absorbs the impact and continues forward: 3/3 successful landings, compared with 0/3 for Vanilla. Platform landings were not included in training.

Simulation Results

Across 25 payload–terrain combinations covering five terrains and five base-mass scales, CARO achieves 87.1% mean success. Each combination uses 1,024 independently initialized rollouts; success means traversing 10 m within 15 seconds without falling.

Adapting to a sudden payload change

When torso mass suddenly increases from 5.6 to 11.2 kg during walking, CARO stays closer to the forward-velocity command and quickly restores its mean yaw rate toward zero.
